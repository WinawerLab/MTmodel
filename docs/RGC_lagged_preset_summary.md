# The lagged midget/parasol front-end

**File:** `pars/shRgcClassesMidgetParasolLagged.m`
**Status:** the live biological front-end, adopted at the 2026-07-12 change of
scope. It replaced the offset-plus-quadrature preset now in `explore/_archive/`.

This document describes **the front-end only**. How it is validated, how it drives
MT, and what the lesion tests showed are in
[`MODEL_AND_LESIONS.md`](MODEL_AND_LESIONS.md).

Figures here are generated by `explore/makeLaggedPresetDocFigures.m`, which writes
to `docs/figures/`.

---

## 1. What it is

The original Simoncelli–Heeger (SH) model feeds V1 from an abstract mathematical
basis: spatial derivatives of a filtered image, orders 0 to 3. This preset replaces
that front-end with something you could point at in a retina — populations of
**retinal ganglion cells** — while still driving the same V1 stage.

It defines those populations along three crossed factors:

| factor | levels | what distinguishes them |
|---|---|---|
| **cell type** | midget, parasol | receptive field size and speed: parasol is big and fast, midget is small and slow |
| **polarity** | ON, OFF | ON fires when brightness increases, OFF when it decreases |
| **lag** | 0, 1, 2, 3 frames | identical cells whose responses arrive at staggered delays |

2 × 2 × 4 = **16 classes**. Each one feeds all 10 of V1's spatial-derivative
read-outs, giving a **160-feature** basis. Those features are combined into neuron
responses by a fitted weight matrix (`pars.rgc.combine = 'weights'`, fitted by
`shFitClassV1Weights`).

> **A note on the word "lag".** In this repo "lag" means *only* the population
> delays above — the four values 0 to 3 that define the lagged copies. The time
> axis of a receptive field or a filter plot is called "time", never "lag". These
> were once conflated, including in `shV1Rf`'s header. Both now use "time".

Two things this preset deliberately does **not** do:

- **No ON/OFF spatial offset, and no ON quadrature filter.** The earlier preset
  used these to build direction selectivity in the retina. That mechanism distorted
  V1 orientation tuning and fought the read-out. Here direction selectivity comes
  from the SH steerable V1 read-out, exactly as in the derivative preset. The full
  argument is in `MODEL_AND_LESIONS.md` §6.1.
- **No exotic cell types.** Every channel is a plain centre-surround cell with one
  or two phases. All the temporal sophistication lives in the *combination* of
  lagged copies, never in a single cell.

> **What the midget and parasol labels buy, and only since 2026-08-14.** Fitting
> this 160-feature basis to SH's V1 does not by itself make the labels mean
> anything. That objective carries no information about cell type, and the fit came
> out midget-dominated, which is Maunsell et al. (1990) backwards. The labels
> acquire meaning at MT, through the two-stream mixture `pars.rgc.mtMix` — a
> parasol-only fast stream plus a mixed slow stream, with `alpha = 0.10`. **Any
> claim about cell types from this preset requires that mixture to be set.** See
> `MODEL_AND_LESIONS.md` §2.3.

## 2. Spatial receptive fields

![RGC spatial RFs](figures/fig1_rgc_spatial_rfs.png)

Each class has a **difference-of-Gaussians** receptive field: an excitatory centre
minus a broader inhibitory surround. Parasol cells are twice the size of midgets in
both parts (centre σ 1.6 against 0.8 pixels; surround σ 4.0 against 2.0). ON and
OFF classes share the *same* receptive field. They differ only in which half of the
rectified signal they keep.

The panels plot the true impulse response of the difference-of-Gaussians the model
actually applies (`localDoG` in `shClassV1Basis.m`), which builds the Gaussians
with `mkGaussianFilter` — sum-normalized and truncated at 3σ.

> **The surround is very weak, and this is worth flagging.** Integrating the
> impulse response, the negative lobe is only about **12–13% of the positive lobe**
> (midget 0.124, parasol 0.132), because `surroundWeight` is 0.25 and the surround
> is spread over a much larger area. Real midget and parasol cells have surrounds
> that are a substantial fraction of centre strength — commonly cited ratios are
> far higher than 0.13. So these receptive fields are close to plain low-pass
> centres with a slight edge enhancement, not strongly band-pass centre-surround
> filters. If the surround is meant to do real work, such as contrast normalization
> or attenuating low frequencies, `surroundWeight` probably needs revisiting.

## 3. Temporal filters, and what the lags are for

![RGC temporal kernels](figures/fig2_rgc_temporal.png)

Each class's temporal filter is a **difference of gamma functions**. That gives a
biphasic impulse response: an initial excursion that overshoots and then rebounds
with the opposite sign. Parasol is fast (peak about 1 frame, τ 0.6 and 1.2); midget
is slow (peak about 4 frames, τ 2.0 and 4.0). Both run for 24 frames.

**The lags are the defining idea of this preset.** A lagged copy is the same filter
padded with *d* frames of zeros (top-right and bottom-left panels). Nothing about
the cell changes. Only the time its response arrives changes.

Why that matters: SH's V1 stage wants temporal channels up to 3rd order, which
behave like successive derivatives in time. A single ganglion cell with one or two
phases cannot supply a 3rd-order channel. But **a weighted difference of the same
filter at staggered delays approximates a derivative** — the same trick as a finite
difference. So the high-order temporal structure is built in the *combination*
downstream, while every individual channel stays physiologically plausible.

The bottom-right panel shows all 16 filters as a bank. The ON and OFF rows are
identical: polarity is imposed by rectification after spatial filtering, not by a
different filter.

## 4. The V1 receptive fields this produces

![V1 RFs, lagged preset](figures/fig3_v1_lagged_rfs.png)

Top row: the **space–time** receptive field — a horizontal slice through the field,
position against time — for four V1 neurons with different preferred directions.
Red is excitatory, blue inhibitory, with symmetric colour limits in each panel.

The signature of direction selectivity is the **tilt**: excitatory and inhibitory
lobes displaced in *both* space and time, so the neuron prefers a stimulus that
arrives at one location and then at another. Neurons 1 and 9 (318° and 261°) show
clearly opposite tilts. The bottom row shows the spatial receptive field at each
neuron's peak-energy time, with its oriented ON and OFF subregions.

> **Caveat.** `shV1Rf` returns the **linear** receptive field referred to the
> stimulus. For a biological preset it convolves the class receptive fields and
> filters with the read-out weights, but it *ignores the ON/OFF half-wave
> rectification*, which is nonlinear. These pictures are the linear skeleton of the
> receptive field, not a complete description of what the model computes.

## 5. Comparison with the derivative (SH) preset

![Lagged vs derivative](figures/fig4_v1_lagged_vs_deriv.png)

**Illustrative, not quantitative** — same four neurons, same slice, each panel
scaled independently.

What is shared: both presets produce **oriented, tilted space–time receptive
fields**, with the same qualitative motion-selective structure and matching tilt
direction for each neuron. That is the point of the whole approach. The biological
front-end is not computing something different. It reaches a similar V1 receptive
field by a different route.

What differs:

- **Extent in time.** The derivative preset's receptive field spans 9 frames, which
  is SH's temporal filter length. The lagged biological one spans about 27 frames
  and carries real structure out to about 15. The dashed line marks the
  derivative's span for scale. The biological filters are simply much longer-lived.
- **Compactness.** The derivative field is a clean, near-textbook two-lobed tilted
  kernel. The biological one is coarser, with more scattered secondary structure —
  a consequence of assembling it from 16 fitted channels rather than deriving it
  analytically.

## 6. How well does it match SH's V1?

**Pooled r = 0.984** on held-out stimuli, against about 0.68 for the retired
offset-plus-quadrature preset. From `tests/testClassPathBiological.m`, fitted on 6
stimuli and evaluated on 4 it had not seen:

| held-out stimulus | r |
|---|---|
| random dots, 45°, speed 0.9 | 0.955 |
| grating, 30°, sf 0.13 cyc/px, tf 0.17 cyc/frame | 0.998 |
| grating, 120°, sf 0.16 cyc/px, tf 0.15 cyc/frame | 0.988 |
| random dots, 180°, speed 0.6, contrast 0.8 | 0.934 |
| **pooled** | **0.984** |

Gratings fit better than dots, which stress the temporal basis harder.

**Do not quote 0.984 as a stable property of the preset.** It is a match to the SH
model rather than to data; it hides real spread across neurons (the worst is
0.709); the samples are not independent; and a separate measurement puts the same
population at 0.93–0.95. The four qualifications are set out in
`MODEL_AND_LESIONS.md` §4.2.

## 7. Known gaps

- **The surround is far weaker than in real ganglion cells** (§2 above).
- **The lesion story is narrower than it was once claimed to be.** A conduction
  delay is *not* something SH's basis cannot express, and in this preset a delay is
  approximately a reweighting of the lag channels. A genuine test that the
  biological front-end says something SH cannot — one exploiting the ON/OFF
  rectification SH lacks — is still outstanding. See `MODEL_AND_LESIONS.md` §6.2
  and `docs/TODO.md` §4.
- **The physical scale raises real plausibility questions**, set out next.

### 7.1 Physical units: pixels to degrees, frames to seconds

Simoncelli & Heeger (1998) **Appendix I, p. 761** fixes the units explicitly:

> frequency units are fixed so the peak of the annulus crosses the temporal
> frequency axis at ω_t = 8 cycles/sec and the spatial frequency axes at
> ω_x = 0.5 cycles/deg.

That pins both axes independently, so the mapping is fully determined. In this
codebase the 3rd-derivative spatial and temporal filters have the same shape and
both peak at **0.2148 cycles/sample**. So the model's preferred speed is exactly
**1 pixel per frame**, which SH's convention makes 8 / 0.5 = **16 deg/sec**:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| 1 pixel | **0.430 deg** (25.8 arcmin), i.e. 2.33 pixels/deg |
| 1 frame | **26.9 ms**, i.e. **37.2 frames/sec** |
| 1 pixel/frame | **16 deg/sec** |

Cross-check: SH describe their own normalization pool as tuned to "moderate speeds
(16 deg/sec)", which matches the derived preferred speed exactly.

**What this implies for the biological preset**, and it is not all comfortable:

| parameter | in frames/pixels | in physical units | plausible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| parasol filter peak | 1 frame | 27 ms | yes — real parasol peaks at 20–40 ms |
| midget filter peak | 4 frames | 107 ms | slow — real midget is nearer 50–80 ms |
| midget sign reversal | 14 frames | 376 ms | very slow |
| filter length | 24 frames | 645 ms | very long for a ganglion cell impulse response |
| population lags 0–3 | 0–3 frames | 0, 27, 54, 81 ms | **too long for conduction delay** (a few ms), but in range for lagged LGN cells or delayed inhibition |
| parasol centre σ | 1.6 px | 0.69 deg | large — real parasol centres are 0.1–0.2 deg outside the far periphery |
| midget centre σ | 0.8 px | 0.34 deg | **far too large** — real midget centres are 0.02–0.05 deg |

There are two honest readings of this.

1. The SH model simply operates at a coarse spatial scale, 0.43 deg per pixel. Its
   "ganglion cells" are better understood as coarse-scale channels than as
   individual cells, and matching single-cell receptive field sizes was never on
   the cards.
2. If the biological front-end is meant to carry meaning specific to cell types —
   which is the whole point of the lesion programme — then the midget-to-parasol
   size *ratio* is preserved but the absolute scale is off by roughly a factor of
   ten, and the "lags" are better justified as lagged LGN cells or delayed
   inhibition than as delay in the optic nerve.

Either way the frame rate is now pinned at about 37 Hz, 26.9 ms per frame, so the
temporal parameters can finally be checked against Kling et al. (2020) time
courses. That check is `docs/TODO.md` §6.
